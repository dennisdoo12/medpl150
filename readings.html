Reading Reflection: In Plato’s Cave

In Plato’s Cave, prisoners are chained inside a cave and can only see shadows projected on a wall. Because the shadows are all they have ever known, prisoners mistake them for reality. Plato uses this metaphor to explain how humans can confuse appearances with the truth and that the understanding of the world is limited for the prisoners. There is a reality beyond the cave but the prisoners are in a confined space and trapped in an illusion believing that the shadows are real when they are not.

Susan Sontag uses Plato’s cave to frame photography as a modern version of this problem and argues that photographs are like shadows on the cave wall. They feel real when observing them, but photos do not often tell the full picture. Photographs shape how we see reality but leave out context in a lot of cases. Sontag describes the drawbacks of photographs in the modern world, explaining that we risk becoming like prisoners in the cave accepting photographs rather than questioning what they are showing us, what they hide, and how they influence our world view.

I agree with Sontag’s statement that “Photographs, which fiddle with the scale of the world, themselves get reduced, blown up, cropped, retouched, doctored, tricked out”. Photos across all social media are often distorted by people and highly edited. I have edited photos changing the lighting, aspect ratio, and other various settings. The drawbacks of this is that people will begin to believe everything they see and not question whether photos are real or not. Especially with the deployment of AI, photos can be generated like never before and it is very difficult to tell what is real and what is not. I agree with Sontag and believe that as a society, we need to be careful with photographs and continue to question everything we see and react with solid evidence instead of what we see.

Another quote from Sontag that reallys speaks to me is stated as, “Recently, photography has become almost as widely practiced an amusement as sex and dancing - which means that, like every mass art form, photography is not practiced by most people as an art. It is mainly a social rite, a defense against anxiety, and a tool of power.” I agree with this statement because people have begun to post photographs as amusement and constant happiness. Social media allows people to construct a version of life they want others to see, not necessarily the one they are actually living. Photographs become a way to manage how others perceive us, which connects directly to Sontag’s idea of photography as a “tool of power.” This presentation of reality is similar to Plato’s cave metaphor, where shadows are mistaken for truth. Just as the prisoners in the cave accept appearances as reality, viewers of social media images may accept these idealized photographs as an accurate reflection of someone’s life, without questioning what exists outside the frame. So overall, from Sontag’s paper, I am reminded to continue to question all photos that I see and not simply take it as reality. As long as proper research is done, and solid evidence is provided, I will be able to distinguish which photos are real and understand my reality.
